# AIIP Customer Interviews

## Purpose

This directory records structured customer interviews conducted to validate AIIP customer problems and product hypotheses.

## Principle

Customer interviews must capture what people actually do, not merely what they say they might do.

Behavior is stronger evidence than hypothetical preference.

---

## Interview Flow

Customer Signal
↓
Qualified Lead
↓
Conversation
↓
Interview
↓
Problem Evidence
↓
Solution Test
↓
Willingness To Pay

## Evidence Levels

### Weak Evidence

Examples:

- "That sounds useful."
- "I might use it."
- "AI would be helpful."

These statements indicate interest but do not demonstrate a real customer problem or buying intent.

### Moderate Evidence

Examples:

- Customer describes a recurring problem.
- Customer currently uses multiple tools to solve the problem.
- Customer spends meaningful time researching.
- Customer has previously paid for related information or tools.
- Customer describes a recent situation where the problem affected a decision.

Moderate evidence indicates that the problem may be real and worth investigating further.

### Strong Evidence

Examples:

- Customer provides a specific recent example.
- Customer identifies a measurable consequence of the problem.
- Customer has spent significant time or money attempting to solve the problem.
- Customer tests an AIIP product.
- Customer changes behavior because of the product.
- Customer asks to use the product again.
- Customer agrees to pay.
- Customer actually pays.

Strong evidence should drive AIIP product decisions.

---

## Interview Objectives

Each interview should attempt to discover:

1. What investment decision the customer was trying to make.
2. What triggered the decision.
3. What information the customer used.
4. Which sources the customer trusted.
5. Where information conflicted.
6. How much time the customer spent researching.
7. What tools the customer currently uses.
8. What part of the current process is difficult.
9. What happens when the decision is wrong.
10. Whether the customer already spends money solving the problem.
11. Whether an AIIP solution could improve the existing workflow.
12. Whether the customer is willing to test the product.
13. Whether the customer is willing to pay.

---

## Customer Signal

Identify why the person was selected for research.

Examples:

- Asked what digital asset to buy.
- Asked whether to hold or sell.
- Asked whether to wait.
- Asked how to evaluate an asset.
- Asked how to evaluate an exchange.
- Asked where to deploy investment capital.
- Described difficulty researching an investment.
- Described conflicting investment information.

A social-media post is a signal, not proof that the person is an AIIP customer.

---

## Qualification

Determine whether the person actually participates in digital-asset decision-making.

Record:

- Investor or trader experience.
- Types of assets evaluated.
- Frequency of decisions.
- Recent investment decision.
- Current research process.
- Existing tools.
- Existing spending.

---

## Recent Decision

Focus on an actual recent decision rather than hypothetical behavior.

Ask:

> Walk me through the most recent time you had to decide whether to buy, hold, sell, or wait on a digital asset.

Explore:

- Which asset?
- What triggered the decision?
- What were you considering?
- What information did you need?
- Where did you look?
- How long did the research take?
- What did you ultimately decide?
- What happened afterward?

---

## Problem Discovery

Ask:

> What was the hardest part of making that decision?

Explore:

- Information overload.
- Conflicting information.
- Missing information.
- Lack of trustworthy sources.
- Time required to research.
- Difficulty comparing assets.
- Risk assessment.
- Liquidity assessment.
- Tokenomics analysis.
- On-chain analysis.
- Portfolio allocation.
- Decision uncertainty.

Do not assume the customer has these problems.

Let the customer describe them.

---

## Existing Workflow

Ask:

> If you had to make that decision again today, walk me through exactly what you would do.

Document:

- Websites.
- Exchanges.
- Research platforms.
- AI tools.
- Social media.
- News sources.
- On-chain tools.
- Portfolio tools.
- Trading tools.
- Spreadsheets.
- Manual processes.
- Time required.

The objective is to understand what AIIP would need to replace, improve, or integrate with.

---

## Information Conflicts

Ask:

> Did you encounter information that disagreed with other sources?

If yes, explore:

- What conflicted?
- Which sources disagreed?
- How did you decide which source to trust?
- How long did resolving the conflict take?
- Did the uncertainty affect your decision?

This is important for validating AIIP's information-synthesis hypothesis.

---

## Failure Points

Ask:

> What is the weakest part of your current investment research process?

Then:

> What information do you wish you had before making the decision?

Document the specific problem rather than translating the answer into an AIIP feature.

---

## Consequences

Ask:

> What happens when you make the wrong decision?

Explore:

- Financial impact.
- Opportunity cost.
- Time lost.
- Research effort.
- Loss of confidence.
- Portfolio impact.
- Emotional impact.

Do not request private financial-account information.

Never request:

- Passwords.
- Private keys.
- Seed phrases.
- Exchange credentials.
- Bank credentials.
- Account numbers.

---

## Existing Spending

Ask:

> Do you currently pay for any research, analytics, signals, portfolio tools, market data, or investment information?

If yes, ask:

- What do you pay for?
- Approximately how much?
- Why do you pay for it?
- What problem does it solve?
- What value does it provide?
- What does it fail to provide?

Existing spending is stronger evidence than hypothetical willingness to pay.

---

## AIIP Concept Test

Only after understanding the customer's existing process should AIIP be introduced.

Explain:

> AIIP is an early investment-intelligence platform focused on helping digital-asset investors structure and evaluate investment decisions. We are testing whether a research report that synthesizes relevant market, fundamental, tokenomic, liquidity, risk, and other information can improve the decision process.

Then ask:

> What would you expect a report like that to tell you?

Do not give the customer a list of answers before they respond.

---

## Product Test

If the problem appears genuine, offer a product test.

Ask:

> Would you be willing to evaluate an early AIIP decision-intelligence report for a specific asset and tell us what is useful, missing, or misleading?

Record:

- Accepted.
- Declined.
- Reason.
- Product tested.
- Feedback.
- Requested changes.
- Whether they would use it again.

---

## Willingness To Pay

Do not ask about payment too early.

First establish:

1. The problem exists.
2. The customer currently experiences it.
3. The customer has attempted to solve it.
4. AIIP provides something useful.

Then ask:

> Have you paid for anything similar before?

Then:

> What would make this valuable enough for you to pay for?

After the customer has tested the product:

> If this were available today, would you pay for it?

If yes:

> What price range would you consider reasonable?

Record what the customer actually says.

Do not interpret enthusiasm as willingness to pay.

## Evidence Classification

### Level 0 — No Evidence

- No response.
- Generic social-media engagement.
- Person does not invest in digital assets.
- Problem is unrelated to AIIP.

### Level 1 — Weak Signal

- General interest.
- "That sounds useful."
- "I might use this."
- Hypothetical willingness to pay.

### Level 2 — Problem Evidence

Customer:

- Describes a real problem.
- Provides a recent example.
- Uses existing tools or workarounds.
- Spends time solving the problem.

### Level 3 — Strong Problem Evidence

Customer:

- Experiences the problem repeatedly.
- Identifies measurable consequences.
- Has previously spent money solving the problem.
- Is actively searching for a better solution.

### Level 4 — Product Validation

Customer:

- Tests an AIIP product.
- Finds it useful.
- Requests continued access.
- Changes part of their workflow.
- Wants to use it again.

### Level 5 — Commercial Validation

Customer:

- Agrees to pay.
- Provides payment commitment.
- Actually pays.

Level 5 is the strongest evidence of commercial demand.

---

## Interview Outcome

Each interview should conclude with one primary outcome:

- Problem Not Confirmed
- Problem Confirmed
- Existing Solution Sufficient
- Existing Solution Inadequate
- AIIP Solution Potentially Valuable
- Product Test Accepted
- Product Test Rejected
- Product Test Completed
- Willing To Pay
- Paid

Do not classify an interview as validated merely because the customer is enthusiastic.

---

## Privacy

Record only information necessary for customer discovery.

Never store:

- Passwords.
- Private keys.
- Seed phrases.
- Exchange credentials.
- Bank credentials.
- Payment-card information.
- Unnecessary personally identifiable information.

Customer research should focus on behavior, problems, workflows, and product feedback.

## AIIP Validation Target

AIIP-012 should aim to establish evidence for:

- 20+ qualified customer signals.
- 10+ genuine customer conversations.
- 5+ recurring problem confirmations.
- 3+ product tests.
- 1+ paying customer.

These are validation targets, not assumptions.

The objective is not to reach the numbers artificially.

The objective is to determine whether genuine customer demand exists.
