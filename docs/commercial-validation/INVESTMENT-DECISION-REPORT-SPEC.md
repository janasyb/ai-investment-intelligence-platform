# AIIP Domain Investment Decision Report



**Product:** AIIP Investment Decision Report

**Validation Program:** AIIP-011 Commercial Validation

**Initial Market:** Domain Investors

**Price:** $49

**Delivery:** Manual / Concierge

**Decision Output:** BUY / WATCH / PASS



---



# 1. Purpose



The AIIP Domain Investment Decision Report helps a domain investor decide whether a specific domain is worth acquiring at a specific asking price.



The report converts fragmented market evidence into a structured acquisition decision.



AIIP does not guarantee investment returns.



The report is an intelligence product designed to reduce uncertainty before capital is committed.



---



# 2. Customer



## Primary Customer



An active domain investor who is currently considering acquiring a specific domain.



## Customer Situation



The customer has:



- identified a domain;

- found an asking price or auction;

- considered acquiring it;

- uncertainty about its value;

- uncertainty about potential buyers;

- insufficient time or expertise to conduct the complete analysis independently.



## Decision Being Supported



> "Should I acquire this domain at this price?"



---



# 3. Required Input



The customer should provide:



- Domain name

- Asking price

- Currency

- Marketplace or seller

- Auction status, if applicable

- Acquisition deadline, if applicable

- Any information already known about the domain



Optional:



- Intended holding period

- Intended resale strategy

- Customer's own valuation

- Previous offers

- Comparable domains the customer is considering



---



# 4. Executive Decision



Every report must begin with:



## Decision



**BUY / WATCH / PASS**



## Confidence



**LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH**



## Asking Price



The current acquisition price being evaluated.



## AIIP Maximum Rational Acquisition Price



The highest price AIIP would consider rational based on available evidence.



## One-Sentence Recommendation



A concise explanation of the decision.



Example:



> WATCH â€” The domain has strong commercial characteristics, but the $2,500 asking price appears unsupported by available comparable evidence. AIIP would consider the opportunity more attractive below approximately $1,500.

# 5. Domain Analysis



Evaluate:



## Name Quality



- Length

- Spelling

- Pronunciation

- Memorability

- Simplicity

- Ease of typing

- Potential confusion

- Word quality

- Phrase quality



## Extension



Evaluate:



- TLD quality

- Commercial acceptance

- Brand suitability

- Investor liquidity

- Buyer familiarity



## Brandability



Assess:



- Brand potential

- Professional appearance

- Pronounceability

- Memorability

- Naming flexibility



## Commercial Intent



Determine whether the domain naturally relates to:



- a commercial product;

- a business category;

- a service;

- a technology category;

- an emerging market;

- a consumer category.



---



# 6. Market Demand



Identify potential buyer categories.



For each buyer category evaluate:



- Number of plausible buyer types

- Commercial relevance

- Ability to pay

- Likelihood of needing the domain

- Evidence of existing businesses or products

- Evidence of naming demand



Potential buyer categories may include:



- startups;

- established companies;

- agencies;

- investors;

- developers;

- SaaS companies;

- ecommerce companies;

- professional services;

- emerging technology companies.



Do not claim that a buyer exists unless evidence supports the claim.



---



# 7. Comparable Evidence



Search for relevant comparable evidence.



Possible evidence sources include:



- historical domain sales;

- current domain listings;

- auction results;

- comparable naming transactions;

- businesses using similar names;

- category demand;

- search interest;

- market activity.



For each important comparable record:



- Domain

- Sale price

- Sale date, when available

- Extension

- Relevance

- Source

- Limitations



Comparable evidence must not be treated as automatically equivalent.



Similarity must be explained.



---



# 8. Valuation



Produce three valuation perspectives where evidence permits:



## Conservative Value



Estimated value under a cautious scenario.



## Base Value



Most reasonable estimated market value based on available evidence.



## Upside Value



Potential value under a strong buyer-demand scenario.



## Maximum Rational Acquisition Price



The maximum price AIIP considers rational for the investor.



This is not necessarily equal to estimated fair value.



The acquisition price should account for:



- expected resale value;

- uncertainty;

- holding costs;

- liquidity;

- time;

- transaction costs;

- downside risk.



---



# 9. Liquidity



Evaluate:



## Expected Holding Difficulty



**LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH**



## Expected Exit Channels



Examples:



- direct outbound sale;

- domain marketplace;

- auction;

- broker;

- inbound buyer;

- existing business network.



## Time-to-Sale Considerations



Discuss whether the domain is likely to require:



- short-term resale;

- medium-term holding;

- long-term holding.



Do not present speculative time estimates as facts.



---



# 10. Risk Analysis



Evaluate:



### Valuation Risk



Could the investor be overpaying?



### Demand Risk



Is there sufficient evidence of potential buyers?



### Liquidity Risk



Could the domain be difficult to resell?



### Trademark / Legal Risk



Could the domain create:



- trademark conflicts;

- brand confusion;

- legal exposure;

- regulatory concerns?



Legal concerns must be identified as risks, not presented as legal advice.



### Market Risk



Could changes in the relevant industry reduce demand?



### Naming Risk



Consider:



- negative meanings;

- pronunciation problems;

- geographic limitations;

- cultural interpretation;

- spelling ambiguity.



### Other Risks



Include additional material risks discovered during research.



---



# 11. Investment Thesis



## Bull Case



Explain the strongest reasonable case for acquiring the domain.



Include:



- strongest demand driver;

- strongest buyer category;

- strongest valuation argument;

- potential upside.



## Base Case



Describe the most probable reasonable outcome based on available evidence.



## Bear Case



Describe what could make the investment unsuccessful.



Include:



- weak demand;

- excessive acquisition price;

- poor liquidity;

- legal issues;

- changing market conditions.



---



# 12. Decision Framework



AIIP must evaluate the opportunity using five primary dimensions:



| Dimension | Assessment |

|---|---|

| Asset Quality | LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH |

| Market Demand | LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH |

| Valuation | LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH |

| Liquidity | LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH |

| Risk | LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH |



The final decision must consider the relationship between:



**Asset Quality + Demand + Valuation + Liquidity + Risk**



A high-quality domain can still receive a PASS if the asking price is excessive.



A mediocre domain can still receive a WATCH if the acquisition price is sufficiently low.



---



# 13. Final Recommendation



## Decision



**BUY / WATCH / PASS**



## Maximum Rational Acquisition Price



State the maximum price clearly.



## Recommended Action



Examples:



- Acquire at or below the stated price.

- Negotiate below the stated price.

- Monitor the domain.

- Do not acquire at the current price.

- Pass unless the asking price changes materially.



## Confidence



**LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH**



Explain why.

# 15. Assumptions



Clearly identify assumptions.



Examples:



- estimated buyer demand;

- estimated resale value;

- estimated holding period;

- incomplete comparable data;

- uncertain sales data;

- inferred commercial demand.



Assumptions must not be presented as facts.



---



# 16. Limitations



Every report must disclose material limitations.



Examples:



- limited comparable transactions;

- incomplete marketplace data;

- unavailable private transaction information;

- uncertain buyer intent;

- changing market conditions;

- inability to guarantee resale.



---



# 17. Report Quality Standard



A report is production-quality only if:



- the decision is explicit;

- the asking price is evaluated;

- valuation reasoning is documented;

- comparable evidence is provided;

- potential buyers are identified;

- liquidity is considered;

- risks are documented;

- assumptions are disclosed;

- sources are recorded;

- limitations are disclosed;

- the maximum rational acquisition price is stated.



---



# 18. AIIP Principle



AIIP must not manufacture certainty.



When evidence is weak:



**lower confidence.**



When valuation cannot be justified:



**say so.**



When risks are material:



**surface them.**



When the opportunity is unattractive:



**recommend PASS.**



The purpose of AIIP is not to make every asset look attractive.



The purpose is to help the investor make a better-informed decision.



---



# 19. Commercial Validation



During AIIP-011, the report is produced manually.



No automated valuation engine is required.



No automated investment recommendation engine is required.



No customer-facing AI system is required.



The objective is to determine whether investors will pay for the intelligence before significant product automation is built.
